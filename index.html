Where Gunsynd sits today

Construction is done. The liability clock has started.This is the moment to use it.

Financial close and the EPC Notice to Proceed to PCL Solar were 18 March 2024. Construction finished in November 2025, Gunsynd was entered into AEMO's market systems on 18 November 2025, and Metis has told shareholders commissioning is complete, with commercial operations guided for on or before 30 June 2026 — a date now behind us, with dispatch into the grid already observed. On the standard project sequence — Practical Completion, where care and custody transfers, then the Commercial Operation Date, then a defects liability period that typically runs 12–24 months — Gunsynd reads as at, or only weeks or months past, that handover.

01

Before handover

Any defect a thermal and visual scan finds before care and custody transfers is PCL Solar's to fix, not Metis's — it's still their site.

02

Inside the defects liability period

Once care and custody has transferred, the same class of fault is still the EPC's cost — but only until the defects liability period closes, typically 12–24 months after Practical Completion. That clock is either already running at Gunsynd or is about to start.

03

After it closes

The identical hot joint or bad MC4 connector becomes Metis's cost to remediate — and a module manufacturer product or performance claim needs thermal or EL evidence to substantiate it with the OEM in the first place.

We could not confirm a certified Practical Completion or Commercial Operation Date from public sources — this is built from Metis's own shareholder disclosures and AEMO's registration record, not a site visit. If Practical Completion is still ahead, this is the pre-handover baseline. If it has just passed, this is the first — and easiest — defect claim to raise against PCL Solar.

How it happens

One machine cuts the grass and reads every string.On the same traverse.

01

The inspection is free

The machine has to travel every row to cut it. Fitting thermal and visual sensing to that same traverse costs nothing extra in time or mobilisation — so you get a full string-level scan on every pass instead of once a year from a drone that cannot see under the panel.

02

Mobilisation is the cost, not detection

Goondiwindi is roughly 350 km from Brisbane. Every separately scoped visit is a vehicle, a crew and a day. Collapsing two scopes into one deployment is where the money is — long before anyone argues about recovered megawatt-hours.

03

Continuous beats periodic

Scheduled mowing lets grass climb to its maximum between visits, then cuts it back. A machine that runs continuously holds it at your specified height permanently — a flat vegetation profile instead of a sawtooth, on a site inside Queensland's mapped bushfire prone area.

The cost we take out is the mowing. The value we add is knowing the condition of every string, every pass, all year — and being able to show exactly when the grass was last cut, and how low.

The obligation

In Queensland, vegetation management is written into the code.

The Construction and operation of solar farms Code of Practice 2024 commenced 5 April 2024, approved under the Work Health and Safety Act 2011 and the Electrical Safety Act 2002. It requires site documentation to set out “actions to manage livestock, pest and vegetation management” and how to manage damage that may cause safety issues.

It sets no grass height, no break width and no cutting interval. The standard at Gunsynd is the one you set — and the only way to stand behind it is to show what was actually done, where, and when. Every pass leaves that record automatically.
